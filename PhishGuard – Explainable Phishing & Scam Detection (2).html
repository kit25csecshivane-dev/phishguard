    Scam guide

    Common scams (with Indian examples) and how to respond. These are general safety tips, not legal advice.

    Fake KYC update

    “Your KYC expires today – click the link to update.”

    • Deadline threats and a link
    • Asks for PAN, Aadhaar, card details or OTP

    Do: update KYC only in your bank’s official app or at a branch.

    OTP and UPI collect fraud

    “Enter your UPI PIN to receive your refund.”

    • You never need a PIN or QR scan to receive money
    • Callers asking you to read out an OTP

    Do: decline unknown collect requests and never share an OTP or PIN.

    Parcel or customs fee

    “Your parcel is held. Pay ₹25 to redeliver.”

    • Small fee plus a link
    • You were not expecting a parcel

    Do: track it on the courier’s official website or app.

    Fake job offers

    “Work from home, earn ₹5,000 a day – pay a registration fee.”

    • Unrealistic pay for easy work
    • Fees or deposits to get started

    Do: never pay to get a job; research the employer independently.

    Investment schemes

    “Guaranteed returns – double your money.”

    • Guaranteed or very high returns
    • Pressure from tip groups on messaging apps

    Do: check that the firm is registered with the regulator (SEBI) before investing.

    Prize and lottery claims

    “Congratulations, you won! Pay a processing fee to claim.”

    • You never entered a contest
    • A fee is needed to receive the prize

    Do: ignore and delete it.

    Safer link habits

    • HTTPS only means the connection is encrypted; scam sites use it too.
    • Read the real domain (the last two parts before the first “/”). Brand names in subdomains can be a disguise.
    • Be careful with shortened links and addresses made only of numbers.
    • Type the official address yourself instead of tapping a link in an unexpected message.

    If it already happened

    1. Call your bank right away to block cards, UPI or net banking.
    2. Change passwords for affected accounts.
    3. In India, report at cybercrime.gov.in or the national cybercrime helpline (1930); confirm current details on the official site.

    About the project

    Intelligent Phishing and Scam Detection Using Explainable Threat Analysis · Artificial Intelligence + Cybersecurity

    Abstract

    Phishing and scam messages cause real financial harm, yet most filters just say “blocked” without teaching users why. PhishGuard analyses URLs, emails and SMS text, produces a 0–100 risk score and a classification (Low Risk, Suspicious, High Risk), and explains every finding using evidence taken from the submitted input.

    Objectives

    1. Detect phishing URLs, suspicious emails and scam text.
    2. Explain each result in simple language with real evidence.
    3. Cover common Indian scams: KYC, UPI, parcel fees.
    4. Protect privacy: no storage, no third-party sharing in this web version.

    How detection works

    1. Input is validated (length, format, allowed URL schemes).
    2. URLs are parsed only; text is matched against the rules below.
    3. Each matched rule adds its weight; the total is capped at 100.
    4. 0–29 is Low Risk, 30–59 Suspicious, 60+ High Risk.
    5. The page shows evidence, why it matters, recommended actions and confidence.

    URL checks (IP host +30, punycode +25, “@” trick +25, shortener +15, many subdomains +15, risky ending +10, keywords +15, very long +10) are listed in the results when they fire.

    Machine learning: what is and is not here

    The full-stack version of this project (Python, FastAPI, scikit-learn, database, login) compares Logistic Regression and Random Forest on 15 URL features and blends the model score with these rules. Its current model is trained only on a small synthetic demo dataset, so its metrics say nothing about real-world accuracy. This website contains no ML model and performs no external reputation lookups; it uses the rules only.

    Limitations

    • Keyword and pattern rules can be evaded, and they are mostly English-language.
    • Rule weights are hand-chosen, not tuned on real data.
    • Attachments are judged by file name in the text only; nothing is opened or scanned.
    • False positives and false negatives are possible. Use it as a second opinion.

    Future enhancements

    Real labelled datasets with calibration, per-prediction explanations (SHAP), an SMS/email text classifier, Hindi and regional-language rules, Safe Browsing or VirusTotal lookups, and a browser extension.

    Technology

    This website: HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Full project: React, TypeScript, Tailwind, FastAPI, scikit-learn, PostgreSQL/SQLite, Recharts, Docker.

    Privacy, method & limits

    Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you paste is sent to any server or third party, and nothing is stored. URLs are parsed, never visited.

    Scores come from transparent, hand-weighted security rules (sum of rule weights, capped at 100). This is a heuristic score, not a calibrated probability. Every indicator quotes the exact text or URL part that triggered it. Results are indicators, not proof of fraud, and “no indicators” does not prove something is safe. This web version has no machine-learning model and no external reputation lookup (the full-stack project adds both).

    If you receive a scam, report it at cybercrime.gov.in (India) and inform your bank through its official app or number.
